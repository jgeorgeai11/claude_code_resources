# Are physician self-referrals for designated health services prohibited under Medicare?

**Date:** 2026-03-16

## Answer

Yes. The Stark Law prohibits physicians from referring Medicare patients for designated health services (DHS) to entities with which the physician or an immediate family member has a financial relationship, unless an exception applies (Source: §1877 of the SSA; 42 U.S.C. §1395nn, via ecfr) [Statute]. Violations result in denial of payment, refund obligations, and potential civil monetary penalties (Source: 42 CFR §411.353, via ecfr) [Regulation].

## Regulatory Basis

- **Statute:** Section 1877 of the Social Security Act establishes the prohibition on physician self-referrals for DHS (Source: §1877 of the SSA; 42 U.S.C. §1395nn, via ecfr) [Statute].
- **Regulation:** 42 CFR Part 411, Subpart J codifies the Stark Law requirements, including the list of DHS, definitions of financial relationships, and available exceptions (Source: 42 CFR §411.351–§411.389, via ecfr) [Regulation]. Designated health services are enumerated at 42 CFR §411.351, incorporating specific CPT/HCPCS (Current Procedural Terminology / Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System) codes maintained by CMS (Source: 42 CFR §411.351, incorporating the DHS list at 42 CFR §411.351(a), via ecfr) [Regulation].

## Analysis

### Scope of the prohibition

The Stark Law applies when three conditions are met:

1. A physician (or immediate family member) has a "financial relationship" with an entity — defined as either an ownership/investment interest or a compensation arrangement (Source: 42 CFR §411.354(a), via ecfr) [Regulation].
2. The physician makes a "referral" to that entity (Source: 42 CFR §411.351, via ecfr) [Regulation].
3. The referral is for a "designated health service" payable by Medicare (Source: 42 CFR §411.351, via ecfr) [Regulation].

### Key exceptions

The regulations provide numerous exceptions. CMS has stated in preamble discussion that "the exceptions are intended to permit arrangements that pose a low risk of program or patient abuse" (Source: 69 FR 16054, Physician Self-Referral Final Rule, Mar. 26, 2004, via federal_register) [Preamble]. Common exceptions include:

- **In-office ancillary services** — Services furnished personally by the referring physician, by another physician in the same group practice, or under the direct supervision of a physician in the group practice (Source: 42 CFR §411.355(b), via ecfr) [Regulation].
- **Fair market value compensation** — Arrangements where compensation is set at fair market value and does not vary with the volume or value of referrals (Source: 42 CFR §411.357(l), via ecfr) [Regulation].

Because the in-office ancillary services exception requires "direct supervision" while the fair market value exception does not, a group practice relying on the former faces a stricter operational burden than one relying on the latter (Source: Inference from §411.355(b) and §411.357(l)).

### Penalties for violations

According to CMS guidance, entities that submit claims for DHS furnished pursuant to a prohibited referral must refund amounts collected (Source: Medicare Claims Processing Manual, Ch. 1 §60.4, via cms_iom) [Guidance]. The statute also authorizes civil monetary penalties of up to $15,000 per service and up to $100,000 for circumvention schemes (Source: §1877(g) of the SSA; 42 U.S.C. §1395nn(g), via ecfr) [Statute].

## Caveats

- The Stark Law applies only to Medicare-covered DHS (Source: §1877(a) of the SSA; 42 U.S.C. §1395nn(a), via ecfr) [Statute]. Medicaid self-referral prohibitions exist but vary by state and are not addressed here.
- The 2021 Stark Law final rule significantly revised key definitions including "fair market value" and "commercial reasonableness" (Source: 85 FR 77492, Stark Law Final Rule, Dec. 2, 2020, via federal_register) [Regulation]. Analyses predating this rule may use outdated definitions.
- This analysis does not address the separate Anti-Kickback Statute (Source: 42 U.S.C. §1320a-7b(b), via ecfr) [Statute], which may independently prohibit arrangements that satisfy a Stark Law exception.
- The Stark Law is commonly referred to as the "physician self-referral law" in industry practice (Source: training knowledge — not verified).

## Gaps
